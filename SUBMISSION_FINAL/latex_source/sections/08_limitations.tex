\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\begin{enumerate}\itemsep2pt
  \item \exactmatch's own audited $\alpha,\beta$ do not yet exist---Analysis
  C is the single largest open piece of this paper.
  \pending{458 rows, preregistered and in progress}.

  \item The correlated-error result is verified by simulation against its
  closed form, not fit to real data---no dataset has produced enough
  scorer-wrong events to fit it (0/69 real T-C events).

  \item \scoreboxed's comparator was verified correct on Linux CI, but two
  smaller structural limitations found in the adversarial suite
  (interval/set-notation reordering; unit-word stripping) remain unfixed.

  \item The scorer-choice generalization is deliberately narrow: one
  benchmark in the strong sense. IFEval corroborates without replicating
  (two leniency settings of one verifier, not two independent libraries); a
  second benchmark with two independent scoring libraries was searched for
  and not found.

  \item The bootstrap-based flip-budget interval's asymptotic coverage is
  not proven for this nonlinear, potentially-empty-set estimand.

  \item Naive-Neyman-fails is demonstrated on this paper's own sparse data;
  generalization to other benchmarks' sparsity patterns is untested.

  \item The T-C round's 69 additional items were analyzed for their
  standalone pooling effect but never merged into the canonical
  pipeline---every headline number reflects only the original 350-item
  audit. Merging is mechanical but undone.

  \item \textbf{The underlying audits are thin; the identified-set width
  reported here is partly a statement about that thinness, not only about
  true scorer error.} 15 of 17 qualifying models have zero observed
  false-credit events; a \emph{perfect} scorer at this paper's realistic
  audit sizes still produces a large dominance ratio (Section~\ref{sec:audit-design}).
  The correct reading is ``scorer error has not been pinned down as
  tightly as sampling variability has,'' not ``this scorer is unusually
  error-prone.''

  \item \textbf{This paper assumes a human reader can, in principle,
  determine ground-truth correctness for a MATH-Hard item}---the
  $(\alpha,\beta)$ framework bounds \emph{scorer} error against that
  reference, not against a reader-independent notion of mathematical truth.
  T-D addresses reader error explicitly and finds no concerning breakdown
  point at realistic rates, but the assumption itself is standard in the
  measurement-error literature and is stated here rather than left
  implicit.

  \item \textbf{Several supporting findings are bug reports, not
  methodological contributions.} The Windows-specific comparator failure,
  the empty-identified-set inversion bug, the Horvitz--Thompson pooling
  bias, and a third-party timeout fragility are real and load-bearing for
  this paper's own correctness, but none is a novelty claim; conflating
  ``bugs found and fixed'' with ``results contributed'' would overstate
  this paper's contribution, and it does not make that claim.
\end{enumerate}
