\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The central claim is not that any individual scorer is unusually bad. It is
that \textbf{reported benchmark accuracy is not automatically identified}---the
measurement procedure that produces it is itself imperfect, audited only
partially, and that partial audit is where most of the practically
relevant uncertainty currently lives, exceeding the sampling uncertainty
the field already reports. This reframes audit-budget allocation from an
implementation detail into part of the benchmark-comparison validity
problem itself, with a concrete, tested design consequence: naive
allocation fails exactly where real audits are sparsest, and a corrected
alternative exists. The scorer-mismatch finding extends this one level
further---even the choice of \emph{which} automated criterion counts as
``the verifier'' is a measurable design decision, though our evidence
bounds rather than resolves how much it matters for any specific
comparison whose own audit is still pending.
