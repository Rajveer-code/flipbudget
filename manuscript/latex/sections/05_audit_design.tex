\section{Audit Design}
\label{sec:audit-design}

\paragraph{The formal problem.} Given qualifying models with audit strata
(credited, wrong) and a fixed additional labeling budget $B$, choose the
allocation minimizing total variance of the resulting stratified
estimators---the classical stratified-sampling problem
\citep{neyman1934stratified}, solved by $n_s \propto \sqrt{p_s(1-p_s)}$
(three-line Lagrangian proof, cited not claimed).

\paragraph{The failure mode, found and fixed.} On this paper's real audit
data, 15 of 17 qualifying models have exactly zero observed false-credit
events (17/17 zero false-miss). Plug-in Neyman weights are therefore
near-zero for almost every stratum and concentrate the entire additional
budget on the one or two cells with the largest---and noisiest, since it
comes from a tiny $n$---observed rate. Swept as an actual budget curve on the
real 120-pair audit-only reconciliation layer (Figure~\ref{fig:audit-budget},
Table~\ref{tab:audit-design}): \textbf{naive Neyman allocation stalls at 74\%
unresolved even at $16\times$ the current budget, while simple proportional
allocation reaches 22\%, and a genuine greedy search over the actual discrete
objective (minimize unresolved-comparison count) reaches 40\% at only
$4\times$ budget.} This does not contradict Neyman's optimality proof---it
demonstrates that variance-optimality-given-the-true-$p$ is a poor proxy for
this discrete objective when the planning estimate itself is this
unreliable, a real, quantified, previously-undocumented limitation.

\paragraph{A second, real bug found building the second audit round.} When
designing the 458-row expansion, the four preregistered joint-scorer cells
were found to have sampling fractions differing up to $6\times$ relative to
their population sizes. Naively pooling raw counts across cells feeding the
same target's credited/wrong union---the initial implementation---is biased:
verified numerically at $+444\%$ relative error in a realistic scenario.
Corrected with a Horvitz--Thompson / post-stratified estimator before any
real label was processed; 7 regression tests added, including a
parametrized synthetic-recovery check at four different stratum-prevalence
pairs.

\paragraph{Negative controls (falsifiability).} The framework is not
structurally biased toward reporting more uncertainty regardless of input:
three of five constructed scenarios (a perfect scorer with an adequately
large audit; a scorer audited at this paper's own derived required-$n$)
correctly report \emph{stable}---ratio below 1 (Table~\ref{tab:negative-controls}).
Notably, even a \emph{perfect} scorer at the real audit's median size
($n=11$) still shows $5.6\times$ dominance---the headline finding is a
statement about audit thinness, not necessarily large true scorer error,
which the audit-design contribution directly targets.
