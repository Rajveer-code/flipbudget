\begin{abstract}
Math leaderboards score free-text answers with automated verifiers and check
the verifiers with small human audits. We ask what such an audit can
establish about a \emph{ranking} of models, on the MATH-Hard subset of the
Open LLM Leaderboard (\nNModels{} models, \nNPairs{} pairwise orderings, and a
400-item human audit). The harness runs two verifiers; they disagree on
\nDisRate{} of \nNRows{} responses and reverse \nScorerFlips{} orderings,
\nScorerFlipsSig{} of them clear under both verifiers' sampling error. Used the
standard way---one verifier, error rates estimated per model---the audit
resolves \nSingleComb{} orderings at 95\% coverage. The audit also shows where
verifier error concentrates: on responses where the two verifiers agree, the
transcribed human answer disagrees with them on \nConcErr{} of \nConcN{}
(95\% upper bound \nConcUpper), while the model-specific errors it finds lie
where they disagree. If agreement between the verifiers is equally reliable
for every model, the same audit resolves \nJointComb{} of the \nCeiling{}
orderings that sampling error permits. That assumption does most of the work:
allowing each model's agreement error to vary within twice the pooled bound
leaves \nHetTwo, within five times \nHetFive, and dropping the assumption
leaves \nNoPool; reaching \nJointComb{} without it would take on the order of
\nNPLabelsFull{} labels. We therefore recommend stratifying audits on joint
verifier outcome, stating the cross-model homogeneity assumption, and spending
part of the audit on testing it; we give a preregistered 458-item design.
Results are on one benchmark, and the audit validates answer extraction, not
semantic equivalence.
\end{abstract}
