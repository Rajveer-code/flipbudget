\subsection{Four-layer uncertainty decomposition}
\label{sec:results-decomposition}

Two of the four layers are easy to conflate and are kept structurally
separate throughout: \emph{scorer-identification-only} asks how much
$A^\ast$ could vary at a fixed, exactly-known $(\alpha,\beta)$ point
estimate, purely from the $\Lambda$-sensitivity allowance for per-item
variation---it would not shrink even with an infinite audit, because it is
not about knowing $(\alpha,\beta)$ better. \emph{Audit-estimation-only} asks
the opposite question: how much does not knowing $(\alpha,\beta)$ precisely
(a finite-sample Wilson confidence interval around the point estimate)
widen the identified set---this layer shrinks toward zero as the audit
grows. The two are computed independently in Table~\ref{tab:layers} and are
never summed into a single ``scorer uncertainty'' figure without saying
which of the two is meant.

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.75\linewidth]{fig1_uncertainty_decomposition.png}
  \caption{Four measured uncertainty sources on the same 136 real MATH-Hard
  pairs. Audit-estimation uncertainty (finite human-audit sample) is the
  largest measured source, $\sim\!4\times$ the sampling width and
  $\sim\!4\times$ the scorer-sensitivity width.}
  \label{fig:decomposition}
\end{figure}

Sampling (0.043 median width, $n=136$) $<$ scorer-identification
(\scoreboxed, $\Lambda=2$; 0.068, $n=66$) $<$ scorer-choice (0.009, $n=120$)
$\ll$ audit-estimation (\scoreboxed; 0.280, $n=120$), shown in
Figure~\ref{fig:decomposition}; see its caption for the scorer-choice-width
caveat (Section~\ref{sec:results-scorer-mismatch}). The full layer-by-layer
table, including the two \exactmatch\ worst/best-case bounds from
Section~\ref{sec:results-scorer-mismatch}, is Table~\ref{tab:layers}.

\subsection{Scorer-mismatch (established main finding; worst-case bound established, \exactmatch's own audit pending)}
\label{sec:results-scorer-mismatch}

MATH-Hard's harness computes two live scores per item: \exactmatch\ (via
\code{math\_verify})---the field's actual reported metric, and this paper's
own $\hat{a}$ throughout---and \scoreboxed\ (last-\code{\textbackslash boxed\{\}})---what
every audit round has measured. \textbf{Full population (22{,}508 rows, 17
models $\times$ 7 subjects): 3.63\% disagreement (95\% CI [3.39\%, 3.88\%]),
model range 0.60--9.59\%.} Bidirectional, not favoring either scorer
systematically (a 12-category constructed adversarial taxonomy shows
\scoreboxed\ false-misses unboxed ``Final Answer: X'' prose; \exactmatch\
false-credits at least one unit-mismatch case). Full scorer-comparison
figures are in Table~\ref{tab:scorer-comparison}; per-pair point-estimate
shift is Figure~\ref{fig:scorer-choice}.

\textbf{The 0.0091 median ``scorer-choice width'' in Figure~\ref{fig:decomposition}
is an accuracy-substitution point-estimate shift---holding \scoreboxed's
audited $(\alpha,\beta)$ fixed and varying only which accuracy feeds the
identification formula---not a full, symmetric two-scorer
identification-uncertainty comparison}, because \exactmatch\ has no audited
$(\alpha,\beta)$ of its own yet.

\textbf{Worst-case sensitivity, established without new labels}: bounding
\exactmatch's plausible $\alpha$ at $[2.38\%, 26.73\%]$ from the observed
disagreement population sizes (worst case $\approx 8.5\times$ \scoreboxed's
known rate) and running it through the identical $\Lambda=2$ methodology
gives a worst-case median scorer-identification width of \textbf{0.166}---still
below the established audit-estimation median (0.280). \textbf{Verdict B
survives even this adversarial bound} (Figure~\ref{fig:worst-case},
Table~\ref{tab:worst-case}), with an honest cost reported alongside the
favorable comparison: only 10 of 120 pairs remain valid at all in the worst
case, and all 10 are fully unresolved.
\pending{\exactmatch's actual (not worst-case-bounded) audited $\alpha,\beta$,
and its own fully self-consistent identification result, Analysis C}.

\subsection{Generalization beyond MATH-Hard (established: limited, not fabricated)}
\label{sec:results-generalization}

IFEval's \code{strict\_acc}/\code{loose\_acc} confirmed as a second, real,
but structurally different data point (2.60\% disagreement, already
measured; two leniency settings of \emph{one} verifier, not two
independently-implemented libraries). BBH checked directly and does not
qualify (single metric field per record). GPQA/MMLU-PRO/MuSR are
loglikelihood-scored multiple-choice, structurally ruled out.
\textbf{Honest conclusion: confirmed on one benchmark in the strong sense, a
related-but-distinct phenomenon on a second; not yet established as a
general property of automated evaluation.}
