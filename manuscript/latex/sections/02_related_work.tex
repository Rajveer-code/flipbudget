\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related-work}

\paragraph{Partial identification and misclassification.} The identification
strategy here is a direct adaptation of the classical misclassification-bounds
literature \citep{manski1995identification,molinari2008partial}---sensitivity/specificity-style
calibration constants bounding a corrupted binary outcome. We claim no new
mathematics in this layer (class A/B throughout, see the supplementary
novelty audit, Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-novelty}); the contribution is
applying it to benchmark comparison specifically, with an audit-design layer
classical misclassification papers do not address.

\paragraph{Concurrent, independent work.} \citet{chen2026partial} study
partial identification of a \emph{prevalence} from panels of LLM
\emph{raters} whose errors are correlated because the raters share training
lineage---the same classical toolkit, a structurally different problem
(their LLMs are the noisy raters; ours are the \emph{rated} models, scored by
one fixed, non-LLM, deterministic verifier). No overlap in estimand, no
overlap in headline results; cited as the closest neighbor, not a competing
claim.

\paragraph{Benchmark and verifier reliability.} VerifyBench
\citep{li2025verifybench} and CompassVerifier \citep{liu2025compassverifier}
study \emph{which verifier is more accurate}---a different question from
ours (``given a verifier's audited error rate, what can still be concluded
about a comparison''), complementary rather than overlapping. A body of work
independently documents answer-format/extraction dependence as an open
problem in LLM evaluation generally; we do not claim to have discovered that
extraction is fragile, only to formalize its statistical consequence for
benchmark comparison.

\paragraph{Independent, high-value external corroboration.} Hugging Face's
own Open LLM Leaderboard team documented, in a public postmortem
\citep{kydlicek2025fixing}, the same family of comparator limitations this
paper's own adversarial suite independently found for \scoreboxed's
SymPy-based comparison step---no rounding-tolerance support, no
numerical-equivalence support, no variable-assignment support, no matrix- or
set-equivalence support---and switched the leaderboard's own scoring to
\code{math-verify}, the same library this paper's \exactmatch\ field uses,
after finding the old comparator's failures were severe enough to
more-than-double some model families' scores (Qwen) and nearly triple
others' (DeepSeek, whose \code{\textbackslash boxed\{\}}-wrapped answers the
old comparator could not extract at all). We do not claim \scoreboxed's exact
extraction path here is byte-identical to the specific historical scorer HF
retired---only that an independent, production-scale, industry audit of the
same comparator family found failures large enough to force a
platform-wide rescoring of 3{,}751 models, which is direct, external,
non-self-reported evidence that scorer choice is a real, consequential,
previously-underscrutinized problem for exactly this benchmark family---not
a phenomenon manufactured by this paper's own framing. It also means our
\exactmatch\ accuracy input is not an arbitrary alternative scorer: it is
the field's own currently-endorsed replacement metric.

\paragraph{Audit design.} The stratified-allocation solution
(Section~\ref{sec:audit-design}) is \citet{neyman1934stratified}, unmodified---cited, not
claimed novel. The contribution is a documented, tested failure mode of the
plug-in form under the specific sparse-event regime real benchmark audits
actually operate in, and a corrected allocation.

\paragraph{Verification bias.} The stratified-on-scorer-outcome audit design
is checked against, and shown formally distinct from, the classical
partial-verification-bias problem in diagnostic testing
\citep{begg1983assessment}---our sampling probabilities are known by design,
not an unknown behavioral process, which is the condition under which
Horvitz--Thompson estimation \citep{horvitz1952generalization} is valid
rather than biased (Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-proofs}).
